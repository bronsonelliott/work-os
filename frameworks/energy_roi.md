# Energy ROI Framework

Distinguishes energy cost from time cost. Answers: What's sustainable?

## Key Insight

Two tasks can take equal time but different energy. High-impact work can be draining or energizing. Sustainability requires managing BOTH time and energy.

## Analysis

For each activity:
- **Time Cost:** Hours required
- **Energy Cost:** How draining (1-10 scale)
- **Impact:** Value created
- **Sustainability:** Is this sustainable long-term?

## Example

**Task A: Building automation (6 hours, Energy 3)**
- Takes time but energizing, creates leverage
- Sustainable, do more

**Task B: Manual data cleanup (4 hours, Energy 9)**  
- Less time but very draining, low value
- Not sustainable, automate or delegate

**Task C: Executive presentations (3 hours, Energy 2)**
- Low time and energy, high impact
- Very sustainable, do more

## Application

**Quarterly:** Review what drained vs energized you
**Annual:** Assess sustainability of current pace
**Decision-making:** Factor energy cost, not just time

See capture/energy_log.md for tracking patterns.

## Connection to Burnout

Burnout often comes from low-energy-cost work masking low-impact-energy-drain ratio. Fix by:
1. Reduce drain (eliminate, delegate, automate)
2. Increase energizing work
3. Bundle draining with energizing (balance)
